Does My Mom or Dad Hate Me? Why Kids Say 'I Hate My Mom' or 'I Hate My Dad'

Behind 'I hate you' or 'my parent hates me' there is usually a need to feel loved and understood.

When a child says 'I hate you'

Young kids and teenagers often say it when they feel hurt or unseen. Teens pull away from the closest parent while they build independence, so a son may seem to reject his mom, or a daughter her dad, without the bond being broken.

Why hurt builds up

  • Feeling compared or treated worse than a sibling
  • Opening up and getting a lecture back
  • Parents too busy for real conversation
  • Broken promises

What parents can do

  • Say 'I understand why you feel that way' before defending yourself.
  • Ask 'When did you feel most hurt?'
  • Listen without fixing for a few minutes, often.
  • Apologize for what you got wrong.

If you feel your parent doesn't love you

That feeling is real even if you can't know what's in your parent's heart. If it still hurts, talk to a counselor, a trusted adult or a local mental health service. If you are in danger, contact local emergency services. This is general information, not a diagnosis.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my son hate his mom?
Often it's independence-seeking or built-up hurt. Staying steady and listening helps more than forcing closeness.

Why does my daughter say she hates her dad?
She may feel unheard or compared. Ask what hurt and listen first.

What if I feel my mom or dad hates me?
Take your feelings seriously and talk to a counselor or trusted adult.

This article is general parenting information and is not medical or psychological advice or a diagnosis. If you are worried, talk to a pediatrician or a qualified counselor.
