Ballet, Lessons and Gifts for Your Second Child: Keeping Siblings Fair (and Gifts for Other People's Kids)

When one child gets ballet, tutoring or a hot new toy, the others quietly ask, 'What about me?' A simple fairness rule avoids a lot of hurt.

What siblings notice

Kids track who gets time and money. When child two starts ballet, child one and child three wonder whether they'll get a turn.

A fair way to decide

  • Equal opportunity beats equal dollars: can each child choose one activity?
  • Check what older siblings got at the same age.
  • Set a yearly budget and let each child pick within it.
  • Explain 'this year it's her turn; next is yours'.

Gifts and popular toys

  • Match gifts to each child's tastes, not just price.
  • Check age labels and safety marks.
  • Compare prices; hot toys often sell out or rise in cost.
  • Make sure the child actually wants it.

Gifts for a boss's or friend's kids

Choose age-appropriate, safe, modest gifts, and give to all children in the family, not just one. If you're giving to a boss's kids, check workplace gift rules first.

Track gifts and lessons

Record what each child received and what was spent. FairFamily reads photos of gifts and receipts and compares siblings at the same age.

Frequently asked questions

Is it unfair to put only my second child in ballet?
Not if each child has a fair chance to pursue something they love and you can explain the reasons.

Do I need to spend the same on every child?
Equal opportunity and explainable choices matter more than identical amounts.

Can I give a gift to my boss's children?
Keep it modest and age-appropriate, include all kids, and check workplace gift rules.
