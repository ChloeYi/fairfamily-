Favorite Child: Signs a Mom or Dad Has a Favorite and How to Fix Favoritism

Most parents never intend to pick a favorite. Kids still notice patterns, so what counts is how it feels to them.

Why favoritism happens

It's natural to feel closer to a child who shares your temperament, is easier to parent, or fits your routines. The trouble starts when that closeness shows up in time, tone, or rules.

Signs of a favorite child

  • One child gets more smiles and the other more correction
  • The same mistake gets different reactions
  • One child's stories get full attention, the other's get 'later'
  • Mom has one 'kid' and dad has another
  • One child is locked into a role: 'the good one', 'the troublemaker'

How to reduce favoritism

  • Admit it to yourself first; noticing is the start.
  • Track time, praise and spending per child for a week.
  • Spend extra one-on-one time with the child you feel less close to.
  • Drop labels and comparisons.

If a child says you have a favorite

Don't argue. Say 'I hear that it felt that way,' ask for a specific example, and thank them for telling you. Even the favored child can feel pressure.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for parents to have a favorite child?
Feeling closer to one child at times is common. What matters is making sure every child feels valued.

How do I know if my mom or dad favors my sibling?
Look for patterns in time, tone and rules, rather than single events.

How can I stop playing favorites?
Track attention per child, plan one-on-one time and avoid labels like 'the easy one'.
