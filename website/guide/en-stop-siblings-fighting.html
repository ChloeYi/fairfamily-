How to Stop Siblings Fighting: When to Step In and What to Say

You don't have to be the judge. Keep everyone safe, hear each child out, and let the kids help find the fix.

Step in right away when

Separate the kids first and sort out who started it later.

  • Someone could get hurt: hitting, biting, throwing
  • One child is always on the losing end
  • Name-calling or mocking keeps repeating

What to say instead of picking a side

  • 'You both sound really upset' instead of 'Who did it?'
  • Let each child tell their side, one at a time.
  • Ask: 'What could work for both of you?'
  • Praise them when they sort things out on their own.

Prevent the next fight

  • Agree on turn-taking rules before problems start.
  • Give each child a space or belongings that are theirs.
  • Notice if fights spike when a child feels left out, and add one-on-one time.

When it keeps going

If fights last for months or a child seems scared or withdrawn, ask a pediatrician or family counselor for help.

Frequently asked questions

Should I take sides when siblings fight?
Generally no. Acknowledge both feelings and guide them toward their own solution.

Why do my brother and sister fight so much?
Shared space, belongings and parent attention make conflict common, especially for kids close in age.

How do I stop my kids from fighting?
Set turn-taking rules, protect each child's space and time, and step in fast when safety is at risk.
