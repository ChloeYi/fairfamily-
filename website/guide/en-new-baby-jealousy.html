New Baby Jealousy: Helping an Older Sibling Cope With a New Brother or Sister

A new baby changes everything for the first child. Jealousy and baby-like behavior are common ways of asking, 'Do I still matter?'

What it can look like

  • Pinching or pushing the baby
  • Baby talk, clinginess, regression
  • Temporary changes in sleep, eating or toileting
  • More anger or 'I don't like the baby'

How to help

  • Protect a little one-on-one time each day, such as during the baby's nap.
  • Acknowledge feelings: 'It's hard when I hold the baby.'
  • Don't force helper jobs; let them offer.
  • Look at their baby photos together so they know they were just as loved.

When to seek advice

If regression or aggression lasts for months, or your child struggles with sleep, eating or school, talk to a pediatrician or child counselor.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for an older child to regress after a baby arrives?
Yes, temporary regression is common. Extra comfort and one-on-one time usually helps.

How do I help my toddler not be jealous of the baby?
Name the feeling, protect special time and let them help only if they want to.

How long does sibling jealousy last?
It varies by child. Many settle in weeks to months; persistent distress deserves professional input.

This article is general parenting information and is not medical or psychological advice or a diagnosis. If you are worried, talk to a pediatrician or a qualified counselor.
