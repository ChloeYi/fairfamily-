Raising a Son and a Daughter Fairly: Are You Treating Your Boy and Girl Differently?

Without meaning to, we often speak to a son one way and a daughter another. Fair parenting starts with noticing.

Differences that creep in

  • Chores expected of a daughter but not a son
  • Telling a boy not to cry
  • Praise for a girl's looks and a boy's abilities
  • More worry, and more limits, for one child

Questions to ask yourself

  • Would I say this the same way to my other child?
  • Are chores split by age, not gender?
  • Does each child know it's okay to feel sad?

How to parent a boy and a girl fairly

Base decisions on age and personality, not gender. Track allowance, praise and time for each child. If you see a gap, ask yourself whether you can explain it.

Frequently asked questions

Should I raise my son and daughter the same way?
Aim for fewer fixed gender expectations and parent each child by age and temperament.

My daughter says I favor her brother. What do I do?
Ask for specific examples, acknowledge her feelings and look at your own patterns.

How should chores be divided between brother and sister?
Divide by age and ability and rotate tasks.
