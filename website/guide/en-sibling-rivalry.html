Sibling Rivalry: Why It Happens and How Parents Can Help Brothers and Sisters

Rivalry between siblings is normal. It often points to a child's need for attention, so the fix usually starts with how parents share it.

Why siblings compete

Brothers and sisters share parents, space, and belongings. Closer ages tend to mean more overlap in toys and friends. Underneath many arguments is a quiet question: 'Do I matter as much as my sibling?'

Common signs

  • Constant 'it's not fair' complaints
  • Tattling or competing to be the better child
  • Acting out when a sibling gets attention
  • One child pulling away from the other

What tends to help

  • Avoid comparisons like 'Why can't you be like your sister?'
  • Give each child their own space, belongings and one-on-one time.
  • Name feelings: 'You wanted my attention and your brother had it.'
  • Set family rules together, such as taking turns.

When to get extra support

If fights are physical, one child is regularly targeted, or a child seems anxious or withdrawn for weeks, talk to a pediatrician or a child counselor.

Frequently asked questions

Is sibling rivalry normal?
Yes. Some competition and jealousy between siblings is common in many families.

What causes sibling jealousy?
Competing for parent attention, comparisons, and feeling treated differently are common causes.

How do I reduce sibling rivalry?
Give each child one-on-one time, avoid comparisons and explain differences in how you treat them.

This article is general parenting information and is not medical or psychological advice or a diagnosis. If you are worried, talk to a pediatrician or a qualified counselor.
