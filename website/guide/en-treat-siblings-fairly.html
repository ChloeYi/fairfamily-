How to Treat Siblings Fairly: Fair Parenting Without Treating Kids the Same

Fair parenting is about giving each child what they need and being able to explain the difference, not about identical amounts.

Fair does not mean equal

A seven-year-old and a three-year-old can't get the same time, toys, or rules. Fair parenting means meeting each child's needs and being able to explain why things differ, so every sibling feels seen.

Moments kids read as unfair

  • A younger sibling gets something the older one never got at that age
  • One child is corrected more often than praised
  • An older sibling is asked to give in again and again because they are 'the big one'
  • Parent time seems to flow to one child

Practical ways to treat kids fairly

  • Give each child 10-20 minutes of one-on-one time a few times a week.
  • Explain differences out loud: 'Your sister needed me at the doctor today. Tomorrow is your turn.'
  • Compare at the same age: what did your first child get at five, and what does your second get now?
  • Praise effort and strengths specific to each child.

Frequently asked questions

Does treating siblings fairly mean treating them the same?
No. Fair parenting means responding to each child's age and needs and being able to explain differences.

How do I know if I am favoring one child?
Track time, spending and attention per child for a week and compare siblings at the same age.

What if my oldest feels treated unfairly?
Acknowledge the feeling first, add one-on-one time, and reduce 'you're the older one' demands.
